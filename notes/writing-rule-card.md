# Clear Technical Writing: 6-Rule Card for FDEs

A practical reference based on Google's Technical Writing principles and *The Mom Test* discipline, designed for all client and stakeholder communications.

---

### Rule 1: Active voice
* **Principle:** Name who performs the action explicitly.
* **Example:** 
  * *Weak:* "The data is exported from the booking system."
  * *Better:* "Priya exports the data."

---

### Rule 2: One idea per sentence
* **Principle:** Split any sentence that tries to do two jobs into separate, clear statements.
* **Example:** 
  * *Weak:* "Priya exports the data from the booking system and then matches it by hand against the cardiology spreadsheet, which takes most of a day."
  * *Better:* "Priya exports the data. She then matches it by hand against the cardiology spreadsheet. Dana estimates the whole process takes most of a day."

---

### Rule 3: Lead with the point
* **Principle:** The first sentence of a paragraph or message must state its exact purpose. For emails, put the decision or ask right at the top.
* **Example:**
  * *Weak:* "I wanted to follow up on our conversation and share some thoughts on reporting."
  * *Better:* "Decision needed by Thursday: may I speak to Priya for 20 minutes about the no-show export?"

---

### Rule 4: Define each term once and use it consistently
* **Principle:** Spell out acronyms on first use, and pick one single name for an artifact throughout the document. Never alternate between synonyms.
* **Example:**
  * *Weak:* Alternating between "dashboard", "reporting view", and "reconciliation tool".
  * *Better:* Pick one name—such as **"no-show report"**—and use it throughout. Avoid "dashboard" since Dana doubted that was the core problem.

---

### Rule 5: Write for the reader, not yourself
* **Principle:** Avoid the curse of knowledge. Operational managers do not use internal technical jargon; translate technical mechanisms into domain concepts.
* **Example:**
  * *Weak:* "We will build an automated API integration and reconciliation pipeline."
  * *Better:* "We will set up an automatic link between the booking system and the spreadsheet, so nobody has to match the two by hand."

---

### Rule 6: Separate facts from assumptions
* **Principle:** Carry *Mom Test* discipline directly into your writing by explicitly distinguishing verified client statements from unverified team hypotheses.
* **Example:**
  * *Fact:* "Dana said the files never quite match."
  * *Assumption:* "The files probably differ because of entry errors."
